You were right that something was off. The old mark wasn't woven — it was stacked, so each petal sat arbitrarily on top of the last and the gold one ended up buried under everything, chopped into three pieces. This is the same drawing, rebuilt as a true kolam interlace.

What was wrong

Before — stacked
After — woven
A stack, not a weave

Measured off your screenshot: the gold petal was drawn first, so all three ink petals crossed over it and broke it into three separate fragments. The left petal sat on top of everything. Rotate the old mark 90° and the line structure no longer matches itself.

The fix is a pinwheel interlace: every petal passes over its clockwise neighbour and under its anti-clockwise one. Each petal now breaks exactly once, at exactly the same point along its own curve — so the whole mark is identical under a 90° turn, and the gold petal is a single continuous ribbon like every other.

The 13.5% mirror figure is the point, not a flaw: a genuine over-under weave is chiral, so it should differ from its reflection. The old mark reflected almost perfectly, which is what gave it that flat, slightly wrong look.

Construction

Geometry

One petal, rotated 4×90° about the centre. Adjacent petals cross at exactly one point, 26.3 units from centre on each diagonal (circled red). The break is cut at 20.6% along each petal's path — the same number for all four.

Clear space

Keep free space of x on all sides, where x is the diameter of one pulli dot. Never crowd the mark closer than this, and never set it smaller than 20px with the dots showing.

Drawing the muggu

Hand-drawn, not spun

A muggu is laid down by hand, one continuous stroke at a time, and the pulli dots are pressed in as it goes. So the loader draws: each petal opens at the crown, sweeps over the top, comes down the far side into the centre and carries straight on into the short inner stroke — one unbroken pen movement — before slipping under its neighbour to close the loop. Four petals, then the centre, then the four dots, one after another.

Ink carries the structure, gold carries the money. Keep gold to one petal, the dots and the centre — the moment a second petal turns gold the weave stops reading.

Do & don't

Do

Keep the pinwheel direction: every petal over its clockwise neighbour.

Don't

Don't stack the petals flat — the weave collapses and the gold breaks up.

Don't

Don't render the whole mark in gold — there's no contrast to read the crossings.

Don't

Don't skew, stretch or rotate off the square. It's built on a four-fold grid.

Do

Use the reversed cut on plum or any dark ground, never ink-on-dark.
